Technology/Topic

~AI in Transportation~


Artificial intelligence is becoming increasingly integrated into modern society and is now being used in many industries to improve automation and decision-making. One major area where AI is rapidly developing is transportation, particularly through autonomous vehicles, also known as self-driving cars. These vehicles use technologies such as AI, sensors, GPS, and LiDAR systems to operate with minimal human control.

Our group chose this topic because autonomous vehicles are one of the clearest examples of how artificial intelligence can directly impact people’s everyday lives. Autonomous vehicles are also expected to influence industries such as logistics, ride-sharing, public transport, and urban planning. As this technology continues to develop, it raises important social and ethical questions surrounding safety, privacy, employment, accessibility, and AI decision-making.


What are Autonomous Vehicles?:

Autonomous vehicles (also known as AVs) use technology to replace certain parts that would normally require a human such as using AI in navigation to find the fastest route or breaking to avoid obstacles through the use of cameras and sensers.


Levels of autonomy

There are 6 different levels at which the autonomy for an AV can range at. This classification was standardized by SAE international and includes:

  • Level 0: No Automation
    The human driver controls all aspects of driving.
  • Level 1: Driver Assistance
    Basic systems like adaptive cruise control or lane-keep assist control either steering or acceleration/braking, but not both simultaneously.
  • Level 2: Partial Automation
    The vehicle controls both steering and acceleration/braking (e.g., Tesla's FSD Supervised). The driver must remain engaged and ready to take control at all times.
  • Level 3: Conditional Automation
    The car handles all driving tasks in specific conditions. The driver is not required to monitor the environment but must be ready to take over when the system requests it.
  • Level 4: High Automation
    The vehicle can perform all driving tasks and monitor the environment in designated conditions (e.g., specific mapped cities). No human attention is required.
  • Level 5: Full Automation
    The vehicle can operate entirely on its own under all road and weather conditions, without a human driver or passenger ever needed.


How is it used today?

Common technologies include:

  • Lidar and Radar – they emit light pulses and waves to measure the distance and create images of the cars surroundings
  • Cameras – records data to read road signs (speed limit warnings), lane lines (lane departure warnings) and pedestrians or other vehicles
  • Artificial intelligence – Machine learning is used in AVs to optimize route planning or even fuel efficiency modes by optimizing breaking and acceleration


Current State of Autonomous Vehicles

Currently, autonomous vehicles are already operating in selected cities through services such as Waymo’s driverless taxi network, while Tesla continues developing its Full Self-Driving system. Recent developments also show companies increasing investment into autonomous public transport, delivery vehicles, and AI-assisted urban driving technologies.

Future Predictions

In the future, autonomous vehicles are expected to become a major part of transportation systems and everyday mobility. According to Pisarov and Mester (2021), autonomous vehicles may reduce traffic congestion, lower CO₂ emissions, improve convenience through carsharing systems, and increase accessibility for elderly and disabled individuals. The article also explains that many companies are continuing to develop more advanced autonomous systems to improve safety, comfort, and efficiency. However, the authors highlight that there are still major concerns surrounding cybersecurity, software reliability, and public trust, as hackers may potentially gain access to vehicle systems and compromise passenger safety. Overall, although autonomous vehicles offer significant social and technological benefits, further development is still required before they can be fully integrated into society
